# Story arc

The MAYBE deck ran 5-7 minutes and landed because it was built like a film: a question, a problem you
can *see*, a twist, a turn, then the answer working live, and the same question again at the end.

## The 14 beats (template slide in brackets)

| # | Beat | Job | Ground | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Black | Silence before the start. Not counted in the numbering. | black | `uncounted: true`. Start the talk on black. [00] |
| 1 | The question | Make the room think about their own life. Don't answer. | dark | One sentence, one accent word (the verb that is the product: "tried"). [01] |
| 2 | The gap | The size of the problem, as people. | light | 100 people walk in; the share turns accent. One supporting number with a bar. [02] |
| 3 | The human cost | What it does to people. | light | 2 rows: number, pictograms, meaning. Notes say what it doesn't prove. [03] |
| 4 | The other side | Flip to the second audience. | dark | Setup, assumption, beat, "Right?" [04] |
| 5 | The escalation | It's getting worse; the system fights itself. | light | 2 hard numbers left; a phone cycles 4 sourced stories. [05] |
| 6 | Real people | Proof it's lived, not theoretical. | light | No text. A viral post in the middle; real TikToks fly in around it. [06] |
| 7 | The turn | Both problems in one line, then "What if…". | dark | The "What if" is the product's thesis. [07] |
| 8 | Title | The name, revealed by a short intro film. | light | Wordmark at 506px, promise, names, event. [08] |
| 9 | Live demo | The product working, live, in the room. | light | A real voice call, a live prototype, a tool the audience can see respond. [09] |
| 10 | Walkthrough | The full product loop, recorded, 2x speed. | accent | Also the fallback if the live demo fails. [10] |
| 11 | Market | It's big and the buyers want it. | light | Grown bars, proof bars. Separate categories never added up. [11] |
| 12 | Why now | A date that forces change. | light | Timeline draws to the deadline; chips; honest caveat. [12] |
| 13 | Back to the start | Repeat the opening question, then its mirror. | dark | Same words as beat 1 where possible. [13] |
| 14 | Close | Old world, new world, name, two-verb tagline. | light | Ends on the wordmark. Optionally loop the walkthrough after it for Q&A. [14] |

Optional beats from the MAYBE deck: a second live moment (AI agents reviewing the call), a careers or
catalogue browser, the employer side, the business model. Add them after the demo, before the market.

## Rhythm
- Alternate grounds: dark for emotion and questions, light for evidence. Never three dark slides in a row
  except question → twist → turn moments.
- Every dark slide is text only. Every light slide carries one visual that *is* the number.
- 20-40 seconds per slide; the demo gets 60-120. 15-19 slides for a 6-minute pitch.
- The presenter talks over the motion: notes say when to pause ("Pause before 'Right?'").

## Beat sheet (fill and get approved before building)

```
# | beat | headline (accent word in *stars*) | visual | number · source | speaker note (1-3 sentences)
1 | question | How are you supposed to know what you want … if you've never actually *tried* doing it? | – | – | Pause on black first …
2 | gap | Only *26 in 100* graduates are on their ideal career path. | crowd 100/26 + bar 66.8% | ZipRecruiter 2026 grad survey | …
```

## Writing rules
- Headlines are sentences, not labels. "Only 26 in 100 graduates are on their ideal career path." not "Career satisfaction".
- One idea per slide. If you need "and", it's two slides.
- The accent word is the word the presenter stresses out loud.
- Numbers: the figure in mono, the meaning in one plain sentence under it, the source in 15px grey.
- Speaker notes carry the caveat ("an estimate of recommendations, not proven missed hires").
- The question at the start and the end use the product's core verb (tried / show).
